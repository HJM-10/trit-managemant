# TRST Maintenance — website redesign pitch

**Pitch line:** Make the quality of the website match the care customers expect from the business, and make the next enquiry easier.

## The opportunity

Reviewed the live [homepage](https://trst-maintenance.co.uk/) and [about page](https://trst-maintenance.co.uk/about/) on 30 September 2026. Both display “Click edit button” instructions and lorem ipsum. The homepage’s service “More” links and several contact links point to `#`. Its testimonials also contain placeholder language. A large four-field form competes with the headline before the visitor understands the business.

These are visible trust and usability problems. The review does not establish traffic, conversion performance, trading history, or the authenticity of any testimonial. Any commercial uplift from the redesign remains a hypothesis to measure.

## The proposed direction

**Good hands. Great results.** A confident, approachable identity built around navy, electric blue, generous white space and clear typography. Retain recognisable TRST naming and blue while making the page feel intentional and current.

| Existing experience | Proposed experience | Commercial rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic headline and unfinished copy | Specific service positioning, Willenhall location and clear benefits | Help a visitor decide whether TRST is relevant |
| Full enquiry form in the opening screen | One prominent action with a two-step quote journey | Reduce the initial effort to start an enquiry |
| Service links leading to `#` | Six dedicated service detail pages, each leading to a relevant quote | Turn service interest into a useful next step |
| Contact details linked to `#` | Direct phone and email links | Make contacting the business easier, especially on a phone |
| Template testimonials and logo badges | Credible factual information and clear proposed process | Avoid relying on unverified social proof |
| Disconnected page sections | Services → business → process → questions → contact | Answer the visitor’s questions in a deliberate order |

## A three-minute walkthrough

1. **Start at the homepage.** “People should understand what you do, where you are, and how to take the next step within seconds.”
2. **Open the Plumbing & repairs page.** Show useful service information and click **Discuss this service**. The selected service carries into the quote flow.
3. **Complete the demonstration quote.** Show project details first, contact details second, and the transparent preview confirmation. No enquiry is actually sent.
4. **Show the phone layout.** Highlight readable content, expandable navigation, and the persistent call/quote bar.
5. **Close with the commercial proposition.** “This gives your business a more credible first impression and a clearer route from a visitor’s problem to an enquiry. We would measure completed enquiries and calls after launch.”

## What is ready

- Thirteen responsive pages, including individual service pages and searchable FAQs.
- Working desktop and mobile navigation.
- Quote form validation, previous/next steps, service preselection and a request preview.
- Keyboard-accessible native dialogs and disclosure controls, reduced-motion support and a skip link.
- Working phone and email links using details published by TRST.
- Static source without a framework or installation dependency. Google-hosted fonts have local system-font fallbacks.

## Decisions before production

Confirm the real services, service area, operating hours, business history, pricing process, response expectations and qualifications with the business. In particular, do not publish Gas Safe claims without verification. The original site advertises 24/7 availability and 25 years of experience amid unfinished template content; these claims were deliberately not carried into the concept as verified facts.

Replace the original website’s stock-style images with authorised team, van and completed-project photography. Add genuine reviews only after verification and permission. The photographs in this prototype came from the existing website; reuse rights for a public launch have not been independently established.

Connect the form to the agreed inbox or CRM, implement server-side validation and spam protection, and provide an approved privacy notice. Add service-specific pages and local search metadata based on confirmed coverage. Review tracking and consent requirements before adding analytics. The prototype is marked `noindex` and clearly identifies itself as an independent concept.

## Measure the result

Establish a baseline, then compare quote starts, completed enquiries, call clicks and qualified leads after launch. Track form abandonment by step. Do not promise a percentage improvement without evidence.
